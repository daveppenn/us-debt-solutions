What would it mean?

The non-tariff receipts slider excludes tariffs. Tariff receipts are added separately to calculate total revenue, so changing the tariff slider changes the required surcharge without double-counting.

How the rule works

  1. Set the annual federal deficit ceiling to 2% of GDP.
  2. Calculate the projected budget gap above that ceiling.
  3. Apply a uniform percentage reduction to eligible noninterest payments and the same percentage increase to eligible receipts.
  4. Recalculate periodically as fiscal forecasts change; the surcharge reaches zero when the underlying deficit meets the target.

Why protect interest? Failing to honor Treasury obligations risks increasing borrowing costs and damaging financial stability.

Important design questions

A true universal adjustment would affect benefits, salaries, grants, and contracts. Existing legal obligations may require legislation or renegotiation. A recession-sensitive rule could avoid forcing additional austerity in a downturn. Congress would also need to specify how tax refunds, transfers, and non-tax receipts are handled.
