How the rule works

  1. Set the annual federal deficit ceiling to half of the baseline annual deficit.
  2. Calculate the reduction needed to halve the baseline deficit.
  3. Apply a uniform percentage reduction to eligible noninterest payments and the same percentage increase to eligible receipts.
  4. Maintain the equal adjustments until debt held by the public falls to 90% of GDP, then phase both out over 36 months. During recessions, automatically ease both adjustments according to the real-GDP rule.

Why protect interest? Failing to honor Treasury obligations risks increasing borrowing costs and damaging financial stability.

Important design questions

A true universal adjustment would affect benefits, salaries, grants, and contracts. Existing legal obligations may require legislation or renegotiation. Automatic recession relief halves both adjustments after two consecutive quarters of real GDP contraction and suspends them after a 3% four-quarter real GDP decline. After two consecutive quarters of growth, restore them gradually over 12 months. Congress would also need to specify how tax refunds, transfers, and non-tax receipts are handled.

Simplified arithmetic only: assumes proportional responses and no changes in GDP, behavior, interest rates, or underlying spending/revenue forecasts. The surcharge is applied to the existing receipt base, not as a percentage-point increase in tax rates.
